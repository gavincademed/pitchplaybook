The Story To Tell04

The economy at the bottom of the cliff.

This is the story to tell. It reframes the entire business of healthcare, and it lands because providers know it is true.

It starts

A man falls off a cliff. An ambulance rushes him to care. Then another falls, so they build a hospital at the bottom. Then clinics, labs, pharmacies, and device makers all set up shop down there too.

The machine

Before long, an entire economy is thriving at the bottom of the cliff, and all of it runs on one thing: people keep falling. That is our healthcare system. It lives downstream, and it wants to stay downstream, because downstream is where the money is.

The fence

Now someone proposes a fence at the top of the cliff so people stop falling. The businesses at the bottom fight it, because the fence threatens everything they have built. But ask the real question: what is actually better for the patient?

The point

"Medicare is going to spend the money either way, upstream or downstream. Connected Care is the fence. It is upstream money, and it is the only version of this that is better for the patient."

CADE · Upstream money · Connected Care is the fence
The Opener05

Open with questions, not a pitch.

Walk in curious about their world. The fastest way to sell is to show you understand the problem before you offer the fix. Let them name the gap, then position CADE as the answer they basically just described.

"How are you staying connected to your chronic care patients between visits? When a patient's numbers trend bad in week two, do you find out, or do you hear about it at the next appointment?"
Follow with: "How much of your staff's day disappears into refills, lab chases, prior auths, and check-ins?" Then stop talking and let them answer.
Frame the gap

"We should be touching base with these patients between visits, and right now no one is."

Listen for

"We don't hear from them until something goes wrong, or they no-show and slip away."

Sell the feeling, not the code

Providers enroll because they are tired of watching patients fall through the cracks, not because of CPT codes. Lead with that frustration.

Handle The Pushback13

Find the real no fast.

Your time is the asset. Figure out quickly what might hold an account back so you do not chase a dead deal. These two are the big ones, and they kill more deals than anything else.

The #1 killer · Priority

"We're too busy for this."

Ninety-nine percent of accounts are too busy. The real challenge is getting them to prioritize Connected Care at all. Your answer: it is fully turnkey, CADE does the work, and the practice only has to submit the claims we hand them. It adds revenue without adding to their day.

The #2 killer · Billing

"How does the billing actually work?"

Keep it simple. We send the CPT code report at the end of the month, on the first. The practice submits those codes to insurance under their NPI. We send our bill, due Net 60 from that date, which gives them 60 days to collect from insurance first.

"My patients won't stick with it."
As of 2026, partial compliance is billable. Even patients who miss check-ins generate reimbursement, and our care team's whole job is keeping them engaged.
"My patients aren't tech people."
No app, no Wi-Fi, no Bluetooth. Cellular devices that work out of the box. A patient who can plug in a lamp can use these.
"Isn't this just a robocall service?"
The opposite. No AI, no call centers, ever. A dedicated team works your panel every week and your patients get to know them.
